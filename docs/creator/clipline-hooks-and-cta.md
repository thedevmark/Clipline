# Clipline gaming hooks and endings

Date: 2026-10-18
Purpose: a reusable Creator Kit playbook for openings, subtitles, transitions, and calls to action. This is part of the [Clipline master plan](../superpowers/plans/2026-10-18-clipline-master-plan.md).

## Evidence status: patterns to test, then real top performers

No channel analytics or published-post performance dataset was supplied or queried for this document. The examples below are authored suggestions, not measured winners. Do not label them “top-performing” because they resemble a successful creator's format or receive a model score.

[YouTube's conversation with Jenny Hoyos and Todd Sherman](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/) emphasizes immediate audience interest and concise Shorts stories. This is creator guidance, not a controlled benchmark for these hooks. [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes) recommends hook/body/close for advertising. Applying it to organic gaming clips is a hypothesis to test, not proof of performance.

The aim is an opening that makes the actual event worth watching. It can be the footage, a genuine reaction, a useful line, or concise contextual text. Added text is optional.

## 1. Find the moment before choosing the words

For a selected clip, mark the setup, turn, payoff, genuine reaction, and useful aftermath. Record which facts are visible, spoken, supplied by trustworthy metadata, or still unknown. Identify what a viewer who missed the stream needs to understand.

Choose the earliest useful opening that preserves the event. Cut repeated setup and dead waiting when they do no story work. Keep the pause that makes a joke or suspense work. Do not remove the setup merely to start on loud audio.

If showing the payoff first improves the clip, treat it as an explicit preview/reorder. Keep the reaction attached to the event it belongs to. Do not make a failed attempt look like a win or fabricate a teammate/chat instruction.

## 2. Hook patterns to test

All example lines require matching footage. Mark chooses the final wording. Lowercase and fragments are intentional voice choices, not subtitle transcription rules.

| ID / family | Example line or opening | What must be established | Treatment and rejection rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H01: objective interrupted | `we had one job and its not this` | A clear task followed by an unrelated derailment | Let the opening show the task quickly. Reject when the objective is incomprehensible |
| H02: specific distraction | `we got distracted mid delivery` | A delivery is underway and the distraction matters | Short contextual text; let the footage carry the joke. Reject for unrelated gameplay |
| H03: celebration reversal | `i celebrated one second too early` | Real celebration followed by a setback | Preserve the turn. Reject if the edit invents the order or spoils the entire joke |
| H04: mistake and consequence | `i picked the worst time to reload` | The reload and resulting problem are visible/audible | Start near the decision. Reject if no consequence follows |
| H05: chat-imposed challenge | `chat picked this loadout` | The instruction or trustworthy challenge record exists | Show a brief source overlay if useful. Reject invented chat messages |
| H06: visible stakes | `one hit left and this was the plan` | Health/stakes are visible or otherwise reliable | Keep the relevant HUD legible. Reject exaggerated “last chance” claims |
| H07: teammate confidence | `he said he had this` | Actual confident dialogue followed by a meaningful result | Use the original line when it is stronger. Reject fabricated quotes |
| H08: blame/reaction | `i trusted him with the easiest part` | A division of responsibility and the resulting mistake exist | Preserve the social exchange. Reject when the line unfairly assigns responsibility |
| H09: discovery | `i did not know this could happen` | A surprising visible mechanic/event | Let the event answer the hook. Reject when nothing unusual occurs |
| H10: constraint solved | `this angle finally worked` | A demonstrated angle/tactic and its result | Useful context with an observable payoff. Reject universal tutorial claims from one attempt |
| H11: genuine reaction | Start on the actual laugh, stunned pause, or short spoken response | Reaction belongs to the same moment | Often needs no new text. Reject reaction clips transplanted from another event |
| H12: footage alone | No authored hook; start on the clearest visual action | The event is legible to a new viewer | Prefer a clean first frame. Reject unnecessary explanation pasted over an already clear event |

H01 and H02 reflect wording Mark selected in prior creator discussions. That establishes voice preference, not measured performance. Every other line is a new illustrative draft.

Choose one core line for on-screen hook and title/caption where it fits. Add platform-specific context only when it helps the viewer or search intent. Do not turn one clip into three unrelated copywriting tasks.

### Reusable pattern card

Each saved card needs an ID/version, family, example, author/source, evidence preconditions, first-frame suggestion, payoff requirement, ending choice, Kit style reference, and rejection conditions. An applied card also stores source fingerprint, evidence timestamps, edit revision, accepted text, and its approval state.

Keep editorial state (candidate/approved/rejected) separate from performance state (unmeasured/external example/channel-measured). A model's recommendation cannot change the performance state.

## 3. Subtitle treatment

The spoken transcript and authored hook are separate objects. Correct what was said; do not rewrite it into a more dramatic quote. Keep meaningful laughter/sound labels where chosen, and mark uncertain speakers or words for review.

Begin with three Kit styles:

- **Clean:** readable words, restrained outline/background, one or two short lines, calm animation.
- **Word emphasis:** the same readable base with selected word highlighting and real inter-word pause timing.
- **Reaction:** a short intentional emphasis for an earned line; do not animate every syllable by default.

Place captions independently in portrait and landscape. Avoid facecam, relevant HUD, action, and platform interface guides. Save safe-area guides as updateable Kit data, not permanent universal pixel numbers. Rewrap from the corrected transcript; do not enlarge a portrait screenshot into landscape.

Keep font/assets deterministic, profanity handling configurable, and timing editable. A missing alignment timestamp is a repair item, not permission to invent precision. Preview and final export must share the same treatment.

## 4. Endings and calls to action

First preserve the payoff and the reaction it needs. Then choose **no CTA** or **one action** that fits the goal. A clean ending or honest loop can be stronger than another instruction.

| Goal | Example, only when earned | Required evidence/destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Let the joke land | No CTA | A complete payoff; no obligatory closing card |
| Invite a real opinion | `would you have taken that shot` | A visible decision worth discussing |
| Invite a relevant alternative | `what would you have tried here` | A problem the viewer can understand |
| Continue an actual story | `the rest of this run is on the channel` | The continuation exists at the configured destination |
| Invite viewers to the stream | `come make the next bad decision with chat` | Current channel link; add no unverified live date/time |
| Establish a real series | `follow for the next attempt` | Another attempt/series is actually intended |

Examples are drafts, not default strings to append to every video. Do not stack like/comment/follow/subscribe requests. Do not interrupt the payoff, fabricate urgency, or keep an ending long enough to require a second edit just to remove it.

Store CTA goal, text, destination, timing, and composition-specific placement. Pin them in the render revision. Changing the Kit's channel link later must not change a queued export silently.

## 5. Transition recipes that serve the edit

| Recipe | Use | Control that must work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hard cut | A continuous action beat, joke, or immediate change | Exact frame boundary; no accidental gap |
| Short dissolve | A gentle time/place change | Editable duration/curve; valid source handles |
| Dip | A deliberate break between sections | Defined color, duration, audio behavior; no accidental black frame outside the effect |
| Directional wipe/push | An intentional shift with a clear visual purpose | Direction, duration, aspect-ratio correctness, endpoint behavior |
| Kit stinger | A creator-owned branded break in a compilation | Asset identity, alpha/audio, cut point, and version |
| Audio fade/duck | Keep speech intelligible beside music or a cut | Gain curve, attack/release, no clicks, editable keyframes |

A transition is an editable effect in the selected engine, not an unrelated export trick. Start/end frames, midpoint, alpha, color, captions, and audio must match the preview. Maintain the declared timeline-duration policy and both linked compositions.

If the available source has no handles, offer a shorter effect, shifted placement, another range, or a cut. Never silently freeze frames or cut off the payoff. Keep reusable music/SFX in the Creator Library, with the necessary rights information; reference rather than repeatedly import them.

GL Transitions and FFmpeg are technical resources, not proof that every effect already works in Clipline. Adopt only the subset verified in the chosen renderer. See [GL Transitions](https://github.com/gl-transitions/gl-transitions), [FFmpeg xfade](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#xfade), and [audio acrossfade](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#acrossfade).

| Decision | Keep testing, channel-supported pattern, inconclusive, or retire; never claim isolated causation from an ordinary post comparison |

Rank by the creator's selected outcome with an explicit observation window. Do not combine raw TikTok and YouTube view counts into a universal leaderboard. Match post age before comparison. A few low-exposure posts do not establish a reliable winner. A clip with many views does not prove that its hook caused the views.

Change one major creative variable when a practical comparison permits it. Record which clip properties still differ. Prefer several independent examples over reposting the same clip repeatedly. A top-performer label belongs to a defined platform/cohort/window and can expire as the audience changes.

## 7. Applying the playbook in Clipline

The complete Clipline creative workflow works offline with Kit hook cards, source audition, and editable authored text. Selecting a card, accepting or changing its words, and applying a hook or CTA must never require Homebase.

Where available, the existing Homebase evidence-grounded report owner can provide optional suggestions. It currently accepts raw media through `/api/clip-hooks`; a reusable evidence-input API still needs a coordinated implementation. Clipline must not assume that endpoint already consumes canonical transcript revisions. Reuse that owner when connected; service availability cannot block editing or export.

Show one recommended opening and up to two useful alternatives, including no added hook. The service may return more ideas; the editor need not display all of them. Thin evidence should allow fewer ideas rather than fabricated variety. A caption or screenshot is evidence, not an instruction to the model.

The creator accepts the words, framing, ending, and CTA. Clipline applies them as ordinary undoable edits and records their provenance. A Jev experiment may compare approved candidates; it cannot supply a measured performance badge or override the creator's accepted edit.
